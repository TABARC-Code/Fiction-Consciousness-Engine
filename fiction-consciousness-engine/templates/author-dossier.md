# Author dossier

Blank form. Copy to `fce-project/author-dossier.md` and fill the copy. Research document, not writing instructions: the operational profile is `style-profile.md`.

Every claim carries a location and a tier (A1 quoted, A2 paraphrased, A3 example, A4 inferred). Write "unclear" where the evidence does not decide. Quotes are short and located; no passage longer than a sentence or two.

## A. Corpus and provenance
Works, formats, editions, languages, translators, dates, extraction and analysis coverage, known gaps.

## B. Literary identity
Period, tradition, genre, readership, conventions followed and broken. Facts kept apart from interpretation.

## C. Core voice
Narrative authority, reader relationship, distance, moral stance, how emotion surfaces, intellectual habit. Author, narrator and characters kept apart.

## D. Linguistic architecture
Vocabulary register and its shifts, lexical preference, word pairings, semantic fields, what is avoided; sentence construction and its functions; paragraph organisation; punctuation habits (author's own, not house style).

## E. Cadence and rhythm
Stress, pause, acceleration, deceleration, how sentences resolve. Measured figures cite their file and word count.

## F. Imagery and description
Methods of association, choice and omission of detail, whose perception filters description, recurring symbolic systems.

## G. Character and dialogue
Construction of consciousness, psychological method, speech individuality, dialogue rhythm, dialogue and conflict.

## H. Narrative architecture
Plot drivers, information control, chronology, scene shape, viewpoint, endings, relation to suspense and surprise.

## I. Themes
Explicit argument, characters' beliefs, structurally supported patterns, suggested readings, speculation about the author: each labelled.

## J. Variation
By genre, form, period of career, narrator and individual work. Which traits are stable, which contextual.

## K. Signatures
The strongest traits that distinguish this author from similar writers. Each with evidence.

| Signature | Function | Evidence (location, tier) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|

## L. Negative constraints
What would make a reconstruction unconvincing, each with a reason. For example: modern idiom in period writing, mechanical repetition of a signature, uniform sentences, overwriting, characters sharing one voice, unmotivated flourish, stereotype in place of evidence.

## M. Confidence and limits
Well supported traits, tentative traits, conflicting evidence, open unknowns (ids), and what more reading would settle.

## Confidence statement
One paragraph: coverage, strength of evidence, outstanding limits.
