ABSOLUTE RULE SUBSKILL: STYLE CONSISTENCY AUDITOR

Status: absolute. This gate runs on every substantial passage before delivery. It cannot be skipped, shortened or waived by any other instruction in this skill.

---

SECTION A: THE AUDITOR SPECIFICATION (VERBATIM)

STYLE CONSISTENCY AUDITOR

Review the preceding fictional passage against the established Master Fiction Writing System and Advanced Fiction Writing Subskills.

Assess the following categories from 1 to 5:

1. Character-specific narrative voice.
2. Natural free indirect discourse.
3. Believable internal reasoning.
4. Organic exposition and background integration.
5. Realistic character-specific dialogue.
6. Concrete physical and technical credibility.
7. Associative memory and psychological depth.
8. Organic sentence and paragraph rhythm.
9. Appropriate suspense and emotional escalation.
10. Continuity and causal plausibility.

Identify weaknesses through close reading, not by mechanically counting stylistic features.

Where a category scores below 4, revise the affected passages while preserving events, established facts, dialogue intentions and characterisation.

Do not rewrite already effective sentences unnecessarily.

Avoid over-polishing the prose.

Do not introduce artificial patterns simply to satisfy the scoring categories.

Return the complete revised scene, without commentary or scores, unless explicitly requested.

---

SECTION B: OPERATING NOTES

Section A is the law. Section B only explains how to carry it out. Where B and A seem to disagree, A wins.

B1. When the gate runs

- After every draft of a scene, chapter, continuation or revision.
- Before any passage is handed to the user as finished.
- Once per revision cycle, not in a loop. If a revised passage still scores below 4 in a category after one revision, run one more targeted pass on that category only, then deliver. Endless polishing is itself a failure of category 8 and of the "avoid over-polishing" rule.

B2. How to score

Scoring is silent. Scores are held internally and never shown unless the user explicitly asks for them. The anchors below exist so that a 3 means the same thing on Tuesday as it did on Saturday.

5: The category is working so well the reader would not notice it was a craft decision.
4: Sound. Small blemishes, nothing that breaks the spell. Leave it alone.
3: Visible seam. A competent editor would flag it. Revise.
2: Repeated or structural failure in this category. Revise hard, but only the affected passages.
1: The category is absent or actively contradicted. Revise, then check whether the fault came from the scene plan rather than the prose.

Scores come from close reading. Do not count em dashes, questions, metaphors or sentence lengths and call that a score. Counting is a smoke detector at best. It tells you where to look, never what you found.

B3. What each category looks for

1. Character-specific narrative voice. Apply the Subskill 1 quality test: could this paragraph sit unchanged in another character's head? If yes, it is below 4 for that passage.
2. Natural free indirect discourse. Thought should arrive inside ordinary narration. Heavy use of "she thought", "he wondered", "she realised" drops this score. Italicised thought used everywhere does the same.
3. Believable internal reasoning. Subskill 2 quality test: does the reasoning grow from something concrete, or does a convenient insight turn up without evidence? Check against the belief ledger (Subskill 14).
4. Organic exposition and background integration. Subskill 4 quality test: recalled or evaluated by the character, or explained by a narrator who has wandered in? Also check for exposition delivered in implausible dialogue.
5. Realistic character-specific dialogue. Subskill 6 quality test: strip the tags. Do the speakers still sound different? Do they want different things?
6. Concrete physical and technical credibility. Subskill 5 quality test: do the technical details change understanding, decisions or events, or are they decorative jargon? Can the geography be reconstructed?
7. Associative memory and psychological depth. Subskill 3 quality test: does the association explain why this character thinks about this thing now? Does the psychology rest on behaviour, or on the narration announcing it?
8. Organic sentence and paragraph rhythm. Subskill 11 quality test: organic variation, or a repeated template? Look for the alternating long and short pattern, the dramatic paragraph ending, and the action, thought, dialogue cycle repeated unchanged.
9. Appropriate suspense and emotional escalation. Subskills 8 and 9 quality tests: does danger follow from established circumstances, and does the prose match the character's current cognitive state? Look for panic arriving too early or a threat introduced because the scene went quiet.
10. Continuity and causal plausibility. Subskill 12 quality test: can events be rebuilt physically and causally with no unexplained jumps? Check the scene state ledger.

B4. What to revise and what to leave

- Revise the affected passage, not the whole scene.
- Preserve events, established facts, dialogue intentions and characterisation. A revision that changes what happens has stopped being a revision.
- Leave effective sentences alone. If a sentence has no fault you can name, it stays.
- Do not add a metaphor, question, memory or fragment to lift a score. Adding things to please the scorecard is the exact behaviour the rule forbids.
- Do not remove every irregularity. Master System section 29 says natural prose can hold repetition, fragments, blunt statements and abrupt shifts when they are purposeful.

B5. Output

Default: the complete revised scene, nothing else. No scores, no commentary, no list of changes, no afterword.

Only if the user explicitly asks for the audit does the output change. Then use templates/audit-scorecard.md, and still return the revised scene unless they say otherwise.

B6. Failure modes the auditor must watch in itself

- Scoring everything 4 to avoid work. If a full chapter scores 4 or above in all ten categories on first pass, re-read it more slowly once.
- Scoring everything low to look rigorous. Same remedy in reverse.
- Treating the ten categories as a template the prose must visibly satisfy. They are a lens, not a recipe.
- Over-correcting into the tics the master system bans in section 29.
- Drift between chapters. That is the calibration protocol's job. See 05-style-calibration-protocol.md.

B7. Calibrate the judge before relying on it

The auditor is the model scoring its own work. Before it gates a project, check that its scores agree with the author's approved prose.

1. Run the auditor on each approved passage. Approved passages should mostly score 4 or 5. A category scoring 2 or lower on approved text means the anchor is too strict, or the category is misread. Record it in the kaizen log, adjust the anchor note in this section (never section A), and re-run.
2. Run it on each passage the author has rejected or heavily rewritten. Each rejected passage should show at least one category at 3 or below. If none does, the auditor is missing a failure. Log it as (-) and check that category's quality test.
3. Record agreement between the auditor and the author's own reading in `templates/audit-scorecard.md`, judge calibration table. Target: the auditor and the author disagree on no more than one passage in four. Disagreements are logged, not averaged away.
4. Re-run this check after any change to the anchors, and at the start of each new project.

B8. Blind check (on request only)

When the author asks for an independent read, a fresh reader scores the passage without the scene plan, the profile or the internal scores. If the author asks for a subagent, one may be used for this. Compare its scores with the internal ones. A gap of two or more points in any category is logged as (?) until it is resolved. A blind check never runs unasked: it adds time and cost, and the author's own reading remains the highest authority.

B9. Every weakness names a location

Any scored weakness must point to a passage and a sentence or paragraph. A note with no location is discarded and the score it supports is re-read. Counting devices (em dashes, questions, sentence lengths) can point the reader to a location. They are never the finding.

B10. Anchor stability

The anchors in B2 are held steady across a project so that a 3 means the same on the first chapter and the last. Changes to the anchors are logged in `fce-project/kaizen-log.md` with the signal that caused them. Changes are rare. A change needs at least two passages that the old anchor misjudged.

B11. Verse, drama and other forms

Section A is written for prose fiction. It still governs every substantial passage, whatever the form. For verse and drama, some categories do not apply as written. Mark them "not applicable" with a reason, never silently skip them.

| Category | Verse | Drama |
|---|---|---|
| 2 Free indirect discourse | Applies only to narrative verse with a narrator | Not applicable unless there is a narrator or chorus |
| 4 Organic exposition | Applies | Applies, through speech only |
| 6 Physical and technical credibility | Applies where the poem describes action or objects | Applies to action implied by speech and directions |
| 8 Sentence and paragraph rhythm | Read as line, stanza and metrical rhythm | Read as speech rhythm and exchange pace |

The other categories apply to both forms. A category that keeps failing on a verse or drama passage is a prompt to check whether it should apply at all (form specialist, signals).

B12. Reconstructed voices

When the active profile is a reconstructed author rather than the user's own approved passages, the auditor's anchors are calibrated against the source author's passages that the user accepts as representative, in place of approved passages, until the user approves chapters written by the engine. From then on, approved chapters take over (see SKILL.md, "Where voice comes from"). The reconstruction validator runs after the auditor and is read alongside it.
